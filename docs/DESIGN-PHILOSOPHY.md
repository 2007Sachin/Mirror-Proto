# Paper Instrument

*Design philosophy for the MIRROR redesign. Written with the canvas-design method; the visual reference is fanout.sh (type, paper, cards, mono labels), reverse-engineered from its shipped CSS.*

**Paper Instrument** treats a screen as a quiet desk with precise tools laid on it. The ground is warm, almost-white paper. Everything that matters sits on it as a physical object: a card with a hairline edge and a soft contact shadow, a dark pill pressed into the surface, a label set the way a lab technician would stamp it. Nothing glows, nothing floats in a void. The page is meticulously crafted so that it reads as calm before it reads as clever.

**Space and form.** Generous, asymmetric margins. One centred headline carries the page and is surrounded by air; supporting objects drift to the corners, each tilted a degree or two, as if set down by hand. Borders are one pixel and low in contrast. Radius is consistent and modest. Every alignment is the product of painstaking attention: the tilt is the only intentional misalignment, and it is the only one.

**Type as material.** Two voices. A tight, confident grotesque for speech: large, medium weight, negative tracking, never bold-heavy. A pixel monospace for the machine: small caps labels, numbers, and one single word per page set large as a signature. The contrast between a human sans and a bitmap mono is the whole personality. Master-level execution means restraint: the mono word appears once, the labels stay small, the grotesque never shouts.

**Colour.** Paper, ink, and one cold blue. Neutral greys carry nearly everything; blue appears only where something can be pressed or is in progress. Category hues (purple, green, orange) exist for small marks and never for large fields. Dark mode is the same desk at night: warm black, the same hairlines, the same single blue. Contrast is calibrated, not approximated.

**Motion and craft.** Cards lift two pixels on hover and settle. Nothing bounces. Reduced motion removes the lift and keeps the layout. The finished work should look as though countless hours went into the spacing alone, by someone at the top of their field who knew when to stop.
