# Digital Marketing Interview Prep (Indian Startups)

## Overview
Digital marketing hiring at Indian seed-to-growth startups (Swiggy, Nykaa, Meesho-style companies) leans heavily on measurable outcomes rather than brand theory — interviewers probe for comfort with CAC, ROAS, LTV, and funnel math because budgets are tight and every rupee of spend is expected to show a payback path. Most roles are generalist by necessity: a single hire is expected to flex across paid social, SEM, SEO, lifecycle/CRM, and influencer or affiliate channels rather than owning one channel in isolation. Because these companies run on dashboards (Mixpanel, CleverTap, GA4, ad-platform consoles), candidates are tested on data literacy — can you read a funnel, diagnose a metric drop, and translate numbers into a channel decision. Finally, given flat, fast-moving teams, interviews weigh stakeholder fluency (founders, product, sales) and bias toward action over polished long-term brand planning.

## Interview Rounds

### Round 1: Recruiter/HR Screen
**Tests:** Basic fit, communication clarity, and whether career trajectory, compensation expectations, and willingness to work in a high-ambiguity startup environment align with the role.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Walk me through your career so far and why you want to move into/within digital marketing at a startup.
2. Why this company specifically, and why not a larger, more stable brand or agency?
3. What digital marketing channels have you personally owned end-to-end, versus just contributed to?
4. What size of budget have you managed monthly, and at what stage of company?
5. Startups often mean wearing multiple hats — SEO one week, paid social the next. How comfortable are you with that?
6. What's your notice period, and are you interviewing elsewhere currently?
7. What are your compensation expectations, and is there flexibility on fixed vs. variable pay?
8. Tell me about a time a campaign or project failed. What did you learn?
9. How do you stay updated on digital marketing trends and platform changes (e.g., iOS privacy changes, Meta algorithm updates)?
10. What does success look like for you in this role in the first 6 months?

### Round 2: Channel & Campaign Case Study
**Tests:** Hands-on tactical knowledge of paid and organic channels, and the ability to design a realistic, budget-constrained campaign plan rather than recite generic best practices.

**Practice Questions:**
1. You have a monthly budget of ₹15 lakh to launch a new category on our app in two metro cities. How do you split it across Google, Meta, and influencer marketing?
2. Walk me through how you would structure a Google Ads account for a D2C skincare brand selling 20 SKUs.
3. Our Meta ad frequency has crept up and CTR is falling. What creative and targeting levers would you pull?
4. How would you design an ASO (app store optimization) strategy to improve organic installs for a hyperlocal delivery app?
5. A competitor just launched aggressive discount-led campaigns on the same keywords we bid on. How do you respond?
6. Explain how you'd run an influencer/affiliate program for a Tier-2/Tier-3 city expansion with a limited budget.
7. What's your approach to balancing branded vs. non-branded search spend when organic traffic is already strong?
8. How would you use WhatsApp or SMS marketing for re-engagement in a market where email open rates are low?
9. Design a full-funnel campaign (awareness to conversion) for a festive sale (e.g., Diwali) with a two-week runway.
10. If YouTube and Instagram Reels were your only two channels for a video-led launch, how would you allocate spend and measure success differently on each?

### Round 3: Analytics & Metrics (CAC, ROAS, Funnel)
**Tests:** Quantitative rigor — whether the candidate can calculate and interpret core unit economics, diagnose metric movements using funnel breakdowns, and make a scale/pause decision from data rather than gut feel.

**Practice Questions:**
1. If we spend ₹8 lakh on a campaign that drives 4,000 orders at an average order value of ₹600 with a 25% contribution margin, is this campaign profitable? Show your math.
2. Our blended ROAS is 4x but the CFO says only half the attributed revenue is incremental. How do you investigate and present this to leadership?
3. CAC rose 40% month-over-month while conversion rate on the landing page stayed flat. What are the possible causes and how would you isolate them?
4. Walk me through the difference between first-click, last-click, and data-driven attribution, and when you'd use each at a startup with a small data team.
5. How would you calculate LTV:CAC ratio for a subscription-based grocery app, and what ratio would make you comfortable scaling spend?
6. Revenue from a campaign is up 20% but ROAS has dropped from 5x to 3x — would you scale, hold, or cut spend, and why?
7. Break down a hypothetical conversion funnel (impressions → clicks → add-to-cart → checkout → payment success) with sample numbers and identify where you'd focus optimization first.
8. How do you account for iOS 14.5+/ATT tracking limitations when reporting performance marketing ROI?
9. What's a reasonable payback period for CAC in a D2C/quick-commerce business, and how does that change with investor funding stage?
10. If asked to defend your marketing budget in a board meeting with 10 minutes of prep, which 3 metrics would you lead with and why?

### Round 4: Growth & Experimentation
**Tests:** Structured experimentation thinking — hypothesis formation, statistical rigor in A/B testing, and the ability to build a scrappy, prioritized growth roadmap under ambiguity.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Walk me through how you'd design an A/B test for a new onboarding flow, including sample size and how you'd decide significance.
2. Weekly sign-ups dropped 20% with no obvious platform or budget change. How do you diagnose this within 48 hours?
3. How do you avoid declaring a "false win" on an A/B test when a startup is impatient for results?
4. If you joined this company tomorrow with no historical experimentation data, how would you build a 90-day growth experimentation roadmap?
5. What's your framework for prioritizing growth experiments when you have 10 ideas but bandwidth for 2 per sprint (e.g., ICE or PIE scoring)?
6. How would you define and validate a North Star metric for an early-stage marketplace startup?
7. Describe a growth loop (not just a funnel) you'd try to build for a referral-driven consumer app.
8. Activation rate for new users is flat despite acquisition volume doubling. What experiments would you run to improve it?
9. How do you decide when to kill an experiment early versus let it run to full significance, given a startup's urgency?
10. Tell me about a growth experiment you ran (or would design) that failed — what would you change next time?

### Round 5: Content/Brand Strategy
**Tests:** Ability to balance brand-building with performance pressure, craft a coherent positioning and content strategy from scratch, and make judgment calls on tone, voice, and creative direction with limited resources.

**Practice Questions:**
1. How would you build a brand positioning and messaging framework for a new D2C brand with zero existing brand equity?
2. Walk me through how you'd plan content calendars across Instagram, YouTube Shorts, and a blog for a startup with a 3-person content team.
3. How do you decide how much of a limited marketing budget should go to brand-building versus direct-response performance campaigns?
4. Describe how you'd develop visual identity and tone-of-voice guidelines that stay consistent across a growing number of freelancers and agencies.
5. A founder wants punchy, discount-heavy messaging while you believe it dilutes brand equity. How do you navigate this disagreement?
6. How would you adapt brand messaging for a Tier-2/Tier-3 India expansion versus a metro-first launch?
7. What's your approach to measuring brand lift or awareness when you don't have a large research budget?
8. How do you incorporate real-time cultural moments (festivals, cricket, memes) into content without it feeling forced or off-brand?
9. Walk me through a repositioning you'd recommend if a brand's category (e.g., "healthy snacking") suddenly gets crowded with new entrants.
10. How would you structure a UGC (user-generated content) or community strategy to reduce reliance on paid creative production?

### Round 6: Behavioral/Stakeholder Collaboration
**Tests:** Cross-functional working style and resilience — how the candidate handles ambiguity, conflicting priorities between founders/product/sales, and the fast pace and resource constraints typical of Indian startups.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Tell me about a time you had to push back on a founder or senior leader's marketing idea. How did you handle it?
2. Describe a situation where marketing, product, and sales had conflicting priorities for the same quarter. How did you align them?
3. How do you operate when you're given a goal (e.g., "grow installs by 30%") but no clear budget or team to execute it?
4. Tell me about a time you had to make a decision with incomplete data under a tight deadline.
5. How do you handle a situation where a campaign you championed underperforms and leadership questions your judgment?
6. Describe how you've worked with external agencies or freelancers to get quality output on a startup budget and timeline.
7. Tell me about the most ambiguous project you've owned — how did you create structure where there was none?
8. How do you prioritize when every function (sales, product, CX) wants a share of the marketing budget simultaneously?
9. Describe a time you had to deliver a campaign on a significantly compressed timeline due to a sudden business need (e.g., funding announcement, competitor move).
10. How do you build credibility with a technical/product-led founding team as someone from a marketing background?

## Tips
- Come with your own numbers: build a one-page "metrics cheat sheet" (CAC, ROAS, LTV, payback period formulas) and be ready to compute them live on a whiteboard or shared doc — Indian startup interviewers frequently test real-time math, not memorized definitions.
- Research the specific company's stage and channel mix before the interview (check their app store presence, ad library on Meta/Google, recent funding news) so your case-study answers reference their actual constraints rather than generic frameworks.
- Default to scrappy, resource-constrained solutions over "ideal-world" answers — startups reward candidates who can say "here's what I'd do with ₹5 lakh and 2 people in 30 days," not textbook 12-month plans.
- Practice structuring answers around a clear diagnostic framework (e.g., funnel breakdown: spend → traffic → CVR → AOV → retention) so you sound rigorous even when reasoning through a case you've never seen.
- Prepare 2-3 concrete stories that show you've taken ownership end-to-end (not just "assisted with") a campaign, since Indian startup interviewers probe hard on what you personally decided versus what an agency or senior teammate decided.
