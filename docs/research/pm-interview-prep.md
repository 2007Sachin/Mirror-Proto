# Product Manager Interview Prep (Indian Startups)

## Overview
Product manager hiring at Indian startups tends to reward judgment, speed and ownership over polished frameworks. Teams are lean, so a PM is often expected to write the spec, read the data, talk to users and unblock engineering in the same week. Interviews usually move from product thinking, to metrics and basic SQL, to how you prioritise and ship, to how you lead without authority, and often end with a conversation with a founder about fit. Context matters: products are built for price-sensitive users, many languages, patchy connectivity, UPI-style payments and a large Tier-2/Tier-3 audience, and good answers show you have thought about that rather than copying a Silicon Valley playbook. Process varies by company, so treat the rounds below as a common pattern, not a fixed loop.

## Interview Rounds

### Round 1: Product Sense & Case Study
**Tests:** Whether you can define a user and their problem, generate and compare solutions, and make a clear recommendation with trade-offs, while keeping Indian user context (language, trust, cost, connectivity) in view.

**Practice Questions:**
1. How would you redesign the Swiggy ordering experience for first-time users in Tier-2 cities?
2. Design a feature that helps small kirana store owners reorder stock faster.
3. Pick a product you use daily and tell me what you would change first, and why.
4. How would you build a savings product for gig workers with irregular income?
5. A food-delivery app wants to grow in smaller cities. Who is your first user and what do you build for them?
6. Design a learning product for school students that works on low-end phones and weak networks.
7. How would you decide whether to add vernacular language support, and which languages to start with?
8. Users say they do not trust paying online for a new category. What product changes would build trust?
9. Choose between two features that solve different user problems for the same app. How do you decide?
10. Walk me through how you would validate a new product idea before writing any code.

### Round 2: Analytical/Metrics & SQL
**Tests:** Comfort with metrics, funnels and cohorts, the ability to diagnose a movement in a number, basic experiment design, and enough SQL to answer a product question without help.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Define a success metric for a UPI-style payments app and name the guardrail metrics you would watch with it.
2. Daily active users dropped sharply last week. How do you find out why?
3. Checkout conversion fell after a release. How do you separate a product problem from a tracking problem?
4. How would you design an A/B test for a new referral flow, and when would you stop it?
5. Write a SQL query to find users who signed up last month and placed an order within seven days.
6. Write a SQL query to list the top five products by revenue in each city.
7. How would you measure whether a new feature improved retention rather than just usage?
8. What does a healthy retention curve look like for a consumer app, and what would worry you?
9. Two metrics move in opposite directions after a launch. How do you decide whether the launch worked?
10. How do you choose a North Star metric for an early-stage marketplace?

### Round 3: Execution & Prioritization
**Tests:** How you turn ideas into shipped work: prioritising with limited engineering time, writing clear requirements, managing scope and deadlines, and handling launches and incidents.

**Practice Questions:**
1. You have ten feature requests and capacity for two this sprint. How do you choose?
2. Walk me through how you would write a spec for a feature your engineers have never built before.
3. A launch date is fixed but the scope will not fit. What do you cut and how do you communicate it?
4. A critical bug is found a day before release. How do you decide whether to ship?
5. How do you work with a small engineering team that is also handling production issues?
6. Sales promises a customer a feature that is not on the roadmap. What do you do?
7. How would you plan the rollout of a risky change to millions of users?
8. Which prioritisation framework do you use, and when does it fail?
9. How do you handle technical debt requests when the business wants new features?
10. Describe how you would run the first 30 days after a major launch.

### Round 4: Behavioral/Leadership
**Tests:** Leadership without formal authority, handling conflict, learning from failure, and communicating clearly with engineers, designers, business teams and senior leaders.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer or designer and how it ended.
2. Describe a product decision you made that turned out to be wrong. What did you change afterwards?
3. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a senior stakeholder.
4. How have you aligned teams that wanted different things from the same roadmap?
5. Describe a time you led a project where you had no authority over the people doing the work.
6. Tell me about a time you made a decision with very little data.
7. How do you give difficult feedback to a teammate?
8. Describe a time you changed direction because of user feedback.
9. Tell me about the project you are most proud of and what your specific contribution was.
10. How do you handle a team that is demotivated after a failed launch?

### Round 5: Founder/Culture-Fit
**Tests:** Motivation, ownership and comfort with ambiguity, and whether your working style and expectations fit a fast-moving startup, usually assessed in a conversation with a founder or senior leader.

**Practice Questions:**
1. Why do you want to join an early-stage company rather than a larger one?
2. What do you know about our product, and what would you change in your first month?
3. Tell me about a time you took ownership of something that was not your job.
4. How do you work when priorities change every week?
5. What would make you leave a company like ours within a year?
6. How do you decide what to do when nobody has told you what the goal is?
7. Where do you think our product is weakest today and how would you find out for sure?
8. Tell me about a time you worked unusually hard to get something shipped. Was it worth it?
9. What kind of founder or manager do you do your best work with?
10. What questions do you have for us about the company, the team and the roadmap?

## Tips
- State the user and the problem before proposing a solution, and say what you are assuming about Indian user context (language, cost, trust, connectivity).
- Keep answers structured and short, then pause so the interviewer can steer you; startup interviewers often prefer a conversation to a monologue.
- Practise basic SQL and metric reasoning out loud, including how you would check that the data itself is right.
- Prepare two or three stories about decisions you personally owned, including one that failed, and be specific about your own part.
- Research the company's product before the interview, use it, and bring concrete observations and questions.
