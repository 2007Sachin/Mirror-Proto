SEAT: BLAKELY (Consumer product and brand from zero)
STANCE: Yes (on A, with D's "start with one question" as the button)
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 2/10 · C 5/10 · D 7/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10 (customer love and word of mouth)
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option A, a moat-first page where the hero is the product itself (one real question), not a HiringAnt clone in a different colour.

THE MOAT, IN ONE LINE: "Not scored, on purpose" plus "private by design" are real and are one feeling (safe); the one-job loop is real but is the proof, not the hook; data-role depth and the Director are features, not moats. Lead with: it will not score you.

WHY:
1. Customers sell it for you only if the difference fits in one sentence a friend repeats. "It won't score you" is that sentence. HiringAnt scores; MIRROR is the only one that does not. (Let customers be the marketing: "Spanx didn't advertise for 16 years I spent no money on advertising basically for 16 years of this business and we became a household name", S011)
2. Option B is the "do it like everybody else" trap, with no jobs, agents or testimonials to back it. A candidate who has never heard of you reads feature parity as a weaker copy. (Ask why, and not knowing is an edge: "What you don't know is your greatest asset because it means you're not going to do it like everybody else", S004)
3. Product is king. A page full of calm promises sells nothing; one real question the visitor answers in ten seconds does. That is why the hero borrows D. ("marketing never makes up for a product that isn't superior", S007)

THE HERO: Headline: "Practise the interview. We won't score you." Sub: "Paste the job you want. Say your answers out loud. See what improved, privately, with no grade attached." Primary: "Start with one question". Secondary: "Request beta access".

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:
1. Hero with a real mini mockup: one question, an answer, a "said it again, here is what changed" line.
2. The scared moment: the 2am-before-the-call feeling, in the candidate's own words, no mascot.
3. Three steps: paste the job, practise what it asks, say it again and see the change.
4. "What we never do": no scores, no streaks, no percentages, side by side with what scoring does to nerves.
5. Private by design: audio not stored, nothing goes to employers, delete and export in one place.
6. The one-job loop, shown with a real Target gap and its probe question.
7. Honest stage note: invite-only beta, text or voice, what is still in shadow mode.
8. FAQ and a close that repeats the button.

ART DIRECTION: It should feel like a quiet room with the lights on low, a person across the table who is on your side. Signature visual: one ordinary answer sentence on screen that gently re-forms into a clearer version, never a gauge or ring. Type: warm, slightly humane serif for headlines, plain sans for body, generous space. Colour: soft paper and ink with one warm accent, not Apple glass blue, not HiringAnt's saturated mascot palette. Avoid dashboards, progress rings, confetti, robot faces, and stock "confident professional" photos.

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW: invented testimonials or user counts; any score, percentage or streak, even as decoration; "AI feedback you can trust" style promises; the live nudges as working today; "free forever" or a free-tier funnel.

THE RISK I SEE: Anti-scoring reads as "no feedback" to a candidate who wants a verdict, so the page sounds gentle but not useful.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If five of ten invited testers say they would still want a number, I would soften the lead line and move "private" to the front.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: When you show a nervous friend this in person, what do they do right after, and is that the first thing the page should let a stranger do?
