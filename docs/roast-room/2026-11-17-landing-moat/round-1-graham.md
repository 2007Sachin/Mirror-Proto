THE CALL: Build D, a try-it hero with one real question and no signup, with the one-job loop story below it and "Request access" as the second button; kill B outright.

THE MOAT, IN ONE LINE: The one-job loop (paste the job description, see your résumé gaps, get asked about them) is the real moat and the one to lead with; not-scored is the feeling it gives, private is table stakes you state once, a non-repeating interviewer is a feature a visitor cannot verify from copy, and SQL depth is a second page for later.

WHY:
1. Nobody has asked for this page yet. The only evidence of demand is usage by real people, and a handful of testers is not evidence. So the page has one job: find the 5 who want it. "Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?" (S098). A visitor who answers one real question in 30 seconds either feels it or leaves. That is data. A pretty hero is not.
2. Do things that don't scale. "The unscalable things you have to do to get started are not merely a necessary evil, but change the company permanently for the better." (S096) The first 5 testers should be recruited by hand: you message data-role candidates who are interviewing now, and the page only has to survive their click. HiringAnt's page serves a funnel with eight testimonials. You have no funnel. Copying its shape is playing house.
3. Ship the smallest version in weeks. "Launching teaches you what you should have been building." (S142) You have a few days. D reuses the Director you already built. A, with a new visual identity, a comparison table and a proof section, spends the same days on polish for a market you have not tested. A comparison table against scorers also invites a fight over a competitor claim you cannot back.
4. The growth question: the one number is how many visitors answer the question, and how many of those request access. Put a counter on both from day one.

THE HERO: Headline: "Practice for the job you actually want." Sub: "Paste the job description. Answer one real question out loud or in text. Nothing is scored, and nothing is stored." Primary CTA: "Try one question". Secondary CTA: "Request access".

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:
1. Hero with the live question: the proof that it works.
2. Three steps (paste the job, see your gaps, get asked about them): the one-job loop in plain words.
3. What a session looks like: a real, truthful screenshot of the Room and the résumé match.
4. Not scored, on purpose: why, said once, in a calm tone.
5. Private by design: audio never stored, nothing shared with employers, delete and export, matching the privacy audit.
6. Honest stage: pre-beta, invite-only, what works today and what is in shadow mode.
7. FAQ plus a request-access close with a short "what role are you interviewing for?" field.

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW:
1. No testimonials, user counts, "by the numbers" strip, or logos.
2. No comparison table naming competitors as scorers.
3. No jobs board, résumé builder, or agents. They are not built and not your bottleneck.
4. No "AI coach that gets you hired" or any outcome promise.
5. No claim about live nudges (pause and ramble reactions), because they are in shadow mode.

THE RISK I SEE: The no-signup try-it costs real voice and model spend on traffic that will never request access, and you learn nothing about who they are. Cap it at one question per visitor and ask for the target role at the end.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If the founder already has 5 named candidates who have said yes to a beta, the page barely matters and C polished in a day is the right call. Also, if fewer than about 1 in 10 people who finish the question request access after 50 visitors, the pitch is wrong and no layout fixes it.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Who are the first 5 people you will personally message this week, and would they be bummed if MIRROR went away after two sessions?
