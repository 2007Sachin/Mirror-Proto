## Hormozi

SEAT: HORMOZI (Offers, pricing, and sales)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on D with A's moat story underneath)
OPTION SCORES: A 6/10 · B 1/10 · C 4/10 · D 8/10
PILLAR SCORE: 6/10 (Offer strength)
CONFIDENCE: 4/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Build D with A's story below it: the hero is one real practice question with no signup, then the not-scored, private, one-job loop, and the button is "request access".

THE MOAT, IN ONE LINE: Real: the one-job loop (3), carried by the demo (4), with "not scored" (1) and "private" (2) as risk reversal; data-role depth (5) is your avatar, not a moat; lead with the loop, because a negative ("we won't score you") is not a dream outcome.

WHY:
1. Offer first. "nailing the offer is the single strongest lever on your business success" (S114). Value Equation (S115): dream outcome is "walk into the interview for THIS job ready"; likelihood is the demo plus your own words coming back; time delay is one question, now; effort is no signup. B adds none of the four and copies a free-tier funnel you cannot fund. B is dead.
2. The try-it is the lead magnet. "A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem." (S112) One question, one replay line, then the next problem it reveals: "now do it for the job you want." That is your attraction offer. I'd rather you earn 40 access requests from people who tried it than 400 from a pretty hero.
3. Not-scored and private cut fear and effort. They are guarantees in spirit: the risk sits with you. They remove objections for nervous candidates, but they do not make anyone want the product. A only scores 6 because it leads with absence and has nothing to prove likelihood. Starving crowd (S002): data-role candidates with an interview coming have pain and urgency. Say that plainly.
4. One avatar, one product, one channel. "Most businesses die from indigestion, not starvation." (S113) HiringAnt's jobs, agents, builder and banners are indigestion. Do not build them.

THE HERO:
Headline: Practice the interview for the data job you actually want.
Sub: Paste the job description. Answer one real question out loud or in text. Nothing is scored, and nothing is kept without you.
Primary CTA: Try one question
Secondary CTA: Request beta access

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:
1. Hero with live one-question demo: proves it works before any claim.
2. Three steps (paste the job, get asked, say it again): shows the one-job loop.
3. What you will not get: no scores, no percentages, no streaks; the calm promise.
4. Private by design: audio never stored, nothing shared with employers, delete and export (only what the audit confirms).
5. MIRROR vs a generic question list, as a plain table of facts.
6. Honest stage: invite-only, small, here is what is in shadow mode.
7. FAQ and close: handle the obstacles before the ask, then request access.

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW:
- No testimonials, user counts, "joined by" lines, or logos.
- No outcome promises ("get the offer", "ace it"), because you have no results data.
- No scores, percentages, or streaks anywhere, including the mockup.
- No claim about live nudges; they run in shadow mode.
- No free-tier or pricing table; there is no price yet.

THE RISK I SEE: The demo breaks or feels generic on the first question, and the page's one proof turns into the reason people leave.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If one question with no signup costs real money per visitor and cannot be capped, or if fewer than 3 of 10 testers finish a first question, I'd drop to A with a recorded demo.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Out of your handful of testers, how many came back and did a second session on their own, and what did they say it was for?

## Blakely

SEAT: BLAKELY (Consumer product and brand from zero)
STANCE: Yes (on A, with D's "start with one question" as the button)
OPTION SCORES: A 8/10 · B 2/10 · C 5/10 · D 7/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10 (customer love and word of mouth)
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option A, a moat-first page where the hero is the product itself (one real question), not a HiringAnt clone in a different colour.

THE MOAT, IN ONE LINE: "Not scored, on purpose" plus "private by design" are real and are one feeling (safe); the one-job loop is real but is the proof, not the hook; data-role depth and the Director are features, not moats. Lead with: it will not score you.

WHY:
1. Customers sell it for you only if the difference fits in one sentence a friend repeats. "It won't score you" is that sentence. HiringAnt scores; MIRROR is the only one that does not. (Let customers be the marketing: "Spanx didn't advertise for 16 years I spent no money on advertising basically for 16 years of this business and we became a household name", S011)
2. Option B is the "do it like everybody else" trap, with no jobs, agents or testimonials to back it. A candidate who has never heard of you reads feature parity as a weaker copy. (Ask why, and not knowing is an edge: "What you don't know is your greatest asset because it means you're not going to do it like everybody else", S004)
3. Product is king. A page full of calm promises sells nothing; one real question the visitor answers in ten seconds does. That is why the hero borrows D. ("marketing never makes up for a product that isn't superior", S007)

THE HERO: Headline: "Practise the interview. We won't score you." Sub: "Paste the job you want. Say your answers out loud. See what improved, privately, with no grade attached." Primary: "Start with one question". Secondary: "Request beta access".

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:
1. Hero with a real mini mockup: one question, an answer, a "said it again, here is what changed" line.
2. The scared moment: the 2am-before-the-call feeling, in the candidate's own words, no mascot.
3. Three steps: paste the job, practise what it asks, say it again and see the change.
4. "What we never do": no scores, no streaks, no percentages, side by side with what scoring does to nerves.
5. Private by design: audio not stored, nothing goes to employers, delete and export in one place.
6. The one-job loop, shown with a real Target gap and its probe question.
7. Honest stage note: invite-only beta, text or voice, what is still in shadow mode.
8. FAQ and a close that repeats the button.

ART DIRECTION: It should feel like a quiet room with the lights on low, a person across the table who is on your side. Signature visual: one ordinary answer sentence on screen that gently re-forms into a clearer version, never a gauge or ring. Type: warm, slightly humane serif for headlines, plain sans for body, generous space. Colour: soft paper and ink with one warm accent, not Apple glass blue, not HiringAnt's saturated mascot palette. Avoid dashboards, progress rings, confetti, robot faces, and stock "confident professional" photos.

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW: invented testimonials or user counts; any score, percentage or streak, even as decoration; "AI feedback you can trust" style promises; the live nudges as working today; "free forever" or a free-tier funnel.

THE RISK I SEE: Anti-scoring reads as "no feedback" to a candidate who wants a verdict, so the page sounds gentle but not useful.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: If five of ten invited testers say they would still want a number, I would soften the lead line and move "private" to the front.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: When you show a nervous friend this in person, what do they do right after, and is that the first thing the page should let a stranger do?

A visitor who answers one real question in 30 seconds either feels it or leaves. That is data. A pretty hero is not.

A comparison table against scorers also invites a fight over a competitor claim you cannot back.
4. The growth question: the one number is how many visitors answer the question, and how many of those request access. Put a counter on both from day one.

THE HERO: Headline: "Practice for the job you actually want." Sub: "Paste the job description. Answer one real question out loud or in text. Nothing is scored, and nothing is stored." Primary CTA: "Try one question". Secondary CTA: "Request access".

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:

2. Three steps (paste the job, see your gaps, get asked about them): the one-job loop in plain words.
3. What a session looks like: a real, truthful screenshot of the Room and the résumé match.

5. Private by design: audio never stored, nothing shared with employers, delete and export, matching the privacy audit.
6. Honest stage: pre-beta, invite-only, what works today and what is in shadow mode.
7. FAQ plus a request-access close with a short "what role are you interviewing for?" field.

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW:
1. No testimonials, user counts, "by the numbers" strip, or logos.
2. No comparison table naming competitors as scorers.
3. No jobs board, résumé builder, or agents. They are not built and not your bottleneck.
4. No "AI coach that gets you hired" or any outcome promise.

## Buffett

STANCE: Conditional yes (on a trimmed A: moat-first, no breadth, no invented proof)

THE CALL: Option A, cut to five or six sections and built on the one-job loop, with every sentence checked against the privacy audit before it ships.

THE MOAT, IN ONE LINE: Not-scored and private are promises a rival with a bigger budget can copy by changing a setting and a sentence, so they are the trust layer, not the castle; the loop around one specific job (Target to résumé gaps to probe questions to replays and stories) is the only candidate that gets harder to copy as a user's own data piles up, so lead with that and let the two promises ride beside it.

WHY:

It cannot easily ship a user's job description, gaps, stories and replays already sitting in one place. That stored history is the switching cost. This is my translation to a consumer product; today it is a hypothesis, not proven.
2. Ruin is a broken promise. "We can afford to lose money but we can't afford to lose reputation." (S028) MIRROR has no revenue to lose, so its only real asset is trust. The audit shows "delete everything" is PARTLY true (sign-in account stays, provider backups) and "nothing shared" must say "no sharing feature". One overclaim on a privacy-led page is the front-page story. That is why ruin is "possible", not "none".
3. Capital efficiency. One founder, a few days. Every day on a page is a day not spent on the product that makes the page true. Option B spends the most days on the least durable thing, a copy of a rival's strengths. I would kill it. D builds a live no-signup demo, which means abuse and speech-provider cost with no revenue; that is a cash question I cannot size from this brief.
4. Circle of competence. (S022) Pre-beta, a handful of testers, no data. Anything that sounds like a result is outside what the founder can know. Stay with what the code does today.

THE HERO: Headline: "Practice the interview for the job you actually want." Sub: "Paste the job description. MIRROR shows where your résumé is thin, then asks the questions that matter. It never scores you, and your audio isn't kept by MIRROR." Primary CTA: "Request access". Secondary: "See how one job becomes practice".

SECTIONS THAT MUST EXIST:
1. Hero with a real product mockup of the Target and gap view: say what it does in ten seconds.
2. The loop in three steps (paste a job, see the gaps, practice the questions): shows the moat.
3. What practice looks like (Director that does not repeat questions, say it again and see what improved): proves it is not a quiz.
4. Not scored, and why: the trust promise in plain words, with the practice-problem exception stated.
5. Private, with limits: audio, sharing, delete and export, and the provider hand-off, copied from the audit.
6. Honest about the stage: invite-only beta, text or voice, what is still in shadow.
7. FAQ and close with "Request access".

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM OR SHOW:
- "Delete everything" with no caveat (sign-in account and provider backups remain), and no "never stored" about audio without "by MIRROR".
- "Answers are never scored" unqualified; SQL problems are marked correct or not.
- Testimonials, user counts, stat strips, or logos; none are real.
- Live nudges (pause and ramble reactions) as working; they run in shadow mode.
- A comparison table naming HiringAnt or others as "scoring you" unless verified on their live product.

THE RISK I SEE: A trust page with one false sentence. Privacy is the whole pitch, so a single overclaim turns the moat into a liability and cannot be taken back once screenshotted.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Evidence that nervous candidates in the first beta cohort cite "not scored" as the reason they came back (say, 5 of 8 testers), which would lift the promise from trust layer to lead. A named, costed cap for option D's demo (under about 50 dollars a month) would raise D.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: If a funded rival added "no scores, no sharing" to its page next month, what would a MIRROR user lose by leaving?
