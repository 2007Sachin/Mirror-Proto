# Roast Room verdict: a landing page built around MIRROR's moat

17 November 2026 · Quick council, two rounds (Round 3 skipped: all four seats converged) · Seats: Hormozi (3), Blakely (3), Graham (2), Buffett (2)
Session files: `docs/roast-room/2026-11-17-landing-moat/`

## Verdict: GO WITH CONDITIONS on option A, trimmed, with a labelled sample try-it in the hero (D-sample)

A moat-first story page: the same kind of rhythm as HiringAnt's (hero with a product moment and two buttons, three steps, feature blocks, honest stage, FAQ, close), but about what only MIRROR does, in a look of its own. No feature-parity page, no jobs board, no stat strip, no testimonials.

Option scores (Round 2, weights Hormozi 3, Blakely 3, Graham 2, Buffett 2): **A 7.1** · D 6.3 · C 4.7 · B 1.0. Council confidence 7 of 10. Every option is a two-way door; Buffett's ruin risk is "possible" and is about one thing only: a false sentence on a privacy-led page.

## The moat (what the page is built on)

- **Real and leading: the one-job loop.** Paste the job description, see where your résumé is thin, get asked about exactly that, say it again, and your stories and replays build up around that job. It gets harder to copy as a user's own history accumulates (Buffett's hypothesis, not yet proven).
- **Real, as the feeling and the guarantee: not scored, and private.** A funded rival can copy a setting and a sentence, so these ride beside the loop (Blakely: they are one feeling, "safe"; Hormozi: risk reversal). They do not lead alone.
- **Features, not moats:** the non-repeating Director, SQL depth. Shown, not headlined.

## Rulings on the four cruxes

1. **Lead.** One headline carries both: "Practise for the job you actually want. Nobody is keeping score." The loop is in the hero (a sample exchange built on a real Target gap), not below the fold. Blakely's feeling and the other three seats' loop share the headline.
2. **The try-it.** D-sample at launch: a clearly labelled sample exchange with a pick-your-own-answer step and a "say it again" that shows what improved, ending on "now do it for the job you want". Zero backend, zero cost, zero abuse surface. Upgrade to a real try-it only when a per-visitor cap, a rate limit and a monthly spend ceiling (about 50 dollars) exist and at least 3 of 10 testers finish a first question. (Hormozi and Graham moved from a real try-it, Buffett from a mockup.)
3. **Comparison.** No table, and no competitor named. A short "a question list versus a list built from your job" block in three plain lines.
4. **Art direction (Blakely, accepted by all).** A quiet room with the lights low and someone on your side. Paper and ink with one warm accent, a humane serif for headlines, plain sans for body, generous space. Signature visual: one ordinary answer sentence that gently re-forms into a clearer one, never a gauge, ring or number. No confetti, robot faces, stock professionals, dashboards, or Apple-glass blue. It must not delay the first button.

## Sections, in order

1. Hero with the sample exchange (ten-second proof).
2. The night-before-the-call moment (names the fear, in plain words).
3. Three steps: paste the job, see the gaps, say it again (the loop).
4. What a session looks like: a truthful picture of the Target gap view and a probe question.
5. Not scored, on purpose, with the SQL exception stated.
6. Private, with limits (audit wording only, below).
7. A question list versus a list built from your job (three plain rows, no rival named).
8. Honest stage: pre-beta, invite-only, what is still in shadow mode.
9. FAQ, then the close with request access and the button repeated.

## Buttons

Primary: **Try a sample question** (Hormozi, Blakely, Graham). Secondary: **Request access** (Buffett wanted these the other way round; the sample is the proof, so it goes first).

## Claims that may appear (checked against `docs/PRIVACY-AUDIT.md`)

- "MIRROR doesn't keep your audio." (add that the speech service hears it live)
- "Your interview answers, spoken or typed, never get a score. Practice problems with one right answer, such as SQL, are simply marked correct or not."
- "MIRROR has no sharing feature. Nothing is sent to employers or recruiters, and nobody else signed in to MIRROR can open your practice."
- "You can delete your practice data and export it. Your sign-in account stays, and backups held by the database provider are outside our control."
- "Your answers and résumé text go to a language-model service for notes and questions."

## What must not appear

Testimonials, user counts, "joined by" lines, logos, a stat strip; outcome promises ("get the offer", "ace it"); any score, percentage or streak, including in the sample; the live pause and ramble nudges as working (they run in shadow mode); a free tier or pricing; a comparison table naming a competitor; "delete everything" without the caveat; "never stored" without "by MIRROR"; a real try-it with no cap.

## Conditions

1. The sample is labelled as a sample ("sample, not your data") and never implies a live model.
2. Every privacy sentence matches the audit wording above, reviewed line by line before launch.
3. No public model endpoint ships without a per-visitor cap, a rate limit and a monthly spend ceiling.
4. Both buttons are counted from day one (sample finished, access requested); if under 1 in 10 who finish the sample request access after 50 visitors, rewrite the headline before touching the layout (Graham, Hormozi).
5. Pre-beta honesty: five named testers recruited by hand matter more than the page (Graham).
